# Runner Journey — v0.1

## Journey map

| Stage | Typical situation | Strategic question |
|---|---|---|
| T-24h to T-1h | preparation | Where will the athlete apply protection and how much? |
| Start | initial application | Is application fast, clean, uniform, and clothing-compatible? |
| 5–10 km | early running | Does the product become unobtrusive once movement and sweat begin? |
| 15–25 km | mid-run | Where does friction/moisture start becoming noticeable? |
| 30–42 km | late marathon | Is protection still adequate after hours of movement? |
| 50–100 km+ | ultra | How do duration, weather, sweat, equipment, and reapplication interact? |
| Finish | transition | Can the skin transition from protection to recovery? |
| Post-run | recovery | Can cleansing and recovery be managed without adding unnecessary irritation? |

## Key insight

Time is not merely a marketing claim. In endurance sports, duration changes the skin environment and the athlete's behavior.

Therefore the product experience should eventually be studied as a timeline rather than as a single moment at application.

## Research questions

- When does the first discomfort appear?
- Which body area fails first?
- Does failure differ by distance?
- How do heat, humidity, rain, and cold change the experience?
- What triggers reapplication?
- What makes an athlete switch between petroleum jelly, anti-chafe products, and tape?
- What is the acceptable tradeoff between durability, feel, residue, and washability?

## Status

Working journey framework. Time points are hypotheses for research, not validated performance standards.
