# Brand Origin — v0.1

## Starting observation

Long-distance runners frequently use petroleum jelly such as Vaseline before running to reduce friction and protect vulnerable skin areas.

The important insight is not that the existing product is ineffective. The insight is that athletes operating in a specialized environment have historically adapted a general-purpose product to that environment.

## Strategic question

> Instead of adapting cosmetics to sport, what if we designed cosmetics from the conditions of sport itself?

## What this story should not become

- Do not frame the brand as simply a better Vaseline.
- Do not imply that existing anti-chafe products do not work.
- Do not claim that all runners have the same failure point.
- Do not turn an observation into an unsupported medical or performance claim.

## Brand-origin hypothesis

The origin story should establish curiosity and a design philosophy rather than attack an incumbent:

1. Observe what athletes actually do.
2. Ask why the solution is used in that environment.
3. Study the environment itself: time, motion, moisture, heat, clothing, equipment, and exposure.
4. Design the skin product from those conditions.
5. Validate the product in the same conditions.
