# Brand Strategy — v0.1

## 1. Brand origin

The starting observation was simple: long-distance runners often apply Vaseline before running. The question was not whether Vaseline works, but why people entering an extreme, specialized environment are still adapting a general-purpose product to that environment.

This leads to the strategic question:

> If we understand the conditions of long-distance exercise first, what would a skin product designed from those conditions look like?

## 2. Problem definition

Endurance exercise exposes skin to multiple simultaneous stresses:

- prolonged repetitive motion
- friction and tissue displacement
- sweat and moisture
- heat and humidity
- clothing and equipment contact
- rain, water, wind, and environmental exposure
- long duration before the athlete can stop and recover

The strategic opportunity is therefore broader than anti-chafe. It is to design skin protection around the actual activity and its timeline.

## 3. First customer

Primary beachhead:

- marathon runners
- ultra-distance runners

Later expansion can include trail running, cycling, triathlon, hiking/outdoor sports, and other activities where duration and repeated motion create meaningful skin-management problems.

## 4. Category direction

Working category:

**Endurance / Performance Skin Care**

The brand should not become known merely as an anti-chafe brand. Anti-chafe is an important function within a larger endurance-skin system.

## 5. Positioning hypothesis

Generic skincare is normally designed around the skin in everyday life. Sports skincare should begin with the conditions created by movement.

Working positioning:

> Skincare designed from the conditions of sport, rather than ordinary skincare adapted to sport.

Working English concept:

> Built around the activity, not adapted to it.

## 6. Core strategic variables

The first product-development lens is:

**TIME × MOTION × ENVIRONMENT**

These variables should eventually connect customer needs to measurable product requirements and GLIDE-SPEC 40 development criteria.

## 7. Brand journey

Long-term product system:

**PROTECT → PERFORM → RECOVER → OPTIMIZE**

This creates a path from pre-activity protection to in-activity performance support and post-activity recovery, followed by personalized or data-driven optimization.

## 8. Relationship to GLIDE-SPEC 40

GLIDE-SPEC 40 is the internal R&D engine. The brand layer defines:

- who the product is for
- what situation matters
- what problem is worth solving
- what performance should mean to the customer

GLIDE-SPEC 40 then translates those decisions into technical targets, formulation candidates, testing, validation, and iterative learning.

## 9. Status

This document is a working strategy. No final brand name, slogan, regulatory claim, superiority claim, or product specification is established here.
