# Korean Runner Evidence — Round 1

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: exploratory / not prevalence evidence

## Purpose

Add Korean/local runner evidence before first-product specification lock. The goal is not to prove market prevalence from web posts, but to test whether the failure modes observed internationally also appear in Korean running contexts.

## Individual cases

### KR-001 — Inner-thigh friction + Vaseline wash-off
- Source: Korean running community (DCInside Running)
- Reported context: shorts change; inner-thigh friction became severe, described as raised stripe-like irritation.
- Existing solution: Vaseline.
- Reported failure: Vaseline reportedly washed away by sweat after about 30 minutes.
- Strategic implication: supports testing sweat exposure and product persistence; also shows garment geometry can be a major causal variable.
- Limitation: one self-report; no controlled timing or objective skin assessment.

### KR-002 — Groin friction at 10 km despite clothing + Vaseline combinations
- Source: Korean running community (DCInside Running), 2026-05-10
- Reported context: 10 km run; shorts, half tights, and combinations with Vaseline were tried.
- Reported failure: friction remained; shorts riding up and tights moving downward were described as important contributors.
- Strategic implication: supports the skin–clothing–motion system model rather than treating topical lubrication as a standalone solution.
- Limitation: one self-report; anatomy, garment fit and running conditions are uncontrolled.

### KR-003 — Vaseline perceived as insufficient beyond ~20 km / 2 h
- Source: Korean running community (DCInside Running), 2022-11-02
- Reported context: runner with prior thigh-chafing experience; long runs.
- Reported observation: Vaseline described as acceptable for shorter use but insufficient after roughly 20 km / 2 hours, leading the user to experiment with longer underwear/tights and different shorts.
- Strategic implication: duration-to-failure should be measured rather than assumed; clothing can be a competing intervention.
- Limitation: older self-report; no matched product comparison.

### KR-004 — Korean marathon guidance explicitly recommends Vaseline for chafing prevention
- Source: Busan International Marathon organizer guide
- Reported guidance: Vaseline is recommended for body chafing prevention, including feet before socks.
- Strategic implication: petroleum jelly is not merely a foreign/community workaround; it is already part of locally published marathon preparation guidance. Therefore differentiation cannot rely on introducing the concept of anti-chafe itself.
- Limitation: organizer guidance is not efficacy research and does not establish current runner prevalence.

## Cross-case interpretation

1. The Korean evidence reproduces three important international hypotheses: sweat-related wash-off/persistence, clothing-motion interaction, and distance/time-dependent failure.
2. It does **not** establish that Vaseline generally fails, nor that a specialized product will outperform it.
3. The most useful next Korean research target is not more generic “does Vaseline work?” discussion, but structured interviews across 10K / half / marathon / ultra with body area, weather, garment/equipment, application timing, failure time, reapplication and willingness-to-pay recorded consistently.

## What this does NOT prove

- prevalence among Korean runners
- objective Vaseline performance limits
- superiority of Body Glide, Squirrel's Nut Butter, 2Toms, or any other incumbent
- a universal time-to-failure threshold
- willingness to pay
- regulatory or medical efficacy claims
