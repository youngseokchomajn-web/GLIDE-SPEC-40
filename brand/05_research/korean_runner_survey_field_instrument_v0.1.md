# Korean Runner Survey Field Instrument — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## 1. Purpose

Convert the approved research protocol into a field-ready questionnaire without leading respondents toward petroleum jelly, specialized anti-chafe products, or a predetermined product format.

This instrument is for customer/problem research. It does not establish cosmetic efficacy, clinical benefit, objective duration, superiority, or regulatory claim eligibility.

## 11. Standardized concept price exercise

Show the same neutral concept card to every respondent. Do not use unsupported numerical performance claims.

Example neutral concept card:

> “A skin-protection cosmetic designed for long-duration exercise. The concept focuses on reducing friction and remaining comfortable during movement, with attention to application convenience and skin compatibility. Exact performance and duration are not specified at this stage.”

Then ask the Van Westendorp-style four questions:

**P1. At what price would this product feel so inexpensive that you would question its quality?** KRW ___

**P2. At what price would it feel like a good-value purchase?** KRW ___

**P3. At what price would it start to feel expensive, but you might still consider buying it?** KRW ___

**P4. At what price would it be so expensive that you would not consider buying it?** KRW ___

Follow with:

**P5. At KRW ___, how likely would you be to purchase it if performance were comparable to your current solution?**
- Definitely would not
- Probably would not
- Not sure
- Probably would
- Definitely would

## 12. Concept trade-off

Randomize 4–6 concept cards. Vary only a controlled subset of attributes:
- persistence
- sweat/water handling
- format
- skin compatibility
- carry/reapplication convenience
- price

Include a **“keep my current solution”** option.

Ask:

**C1. Which option would you choose for your long-distance running?**

**C2. What is the single most important reason for your choice?**
Open text.

Do not show unsupported exact duration claims such as “8 hours” unless those values have already been generated by controlled testing and approved for research use.

## 14. Data-quality rules

- Preserve raw answers and survey version/date.
- Do not silently correct contradictory distance/time or price responses.
- Flag impossible or highly inconsistent values for review.
- Preserve “don't know” separately from missing data.
- Do not deduplicate inconvenient contradictions.
- Record recruitment source and segment quotas.
- Do not report convenience-sample percentages as Korean runner population prevalence.

## 15. Minimum reporting outputs

Before the user-evidence gate is evaluated, report:

1. sample size and screening exclusions
2. body-area frequency by runner segment
3. failure time and distance distributions
4. condition × failure-mode matrix
5. clothing/equipment interaction matrix
6. current solution × switching destination matrix
7. reapplication/carry burden
8. actual annual spend
9. price-sensitivity distribution by segment
10. concept-choice results including keep-current option
11. contradictory/outlier cases
12. qualitative explanations linked to quantitative patterns

## 16. Evidence boundary

Survey findings are customer/problem and market-behavior evidence. They must not be presented as proof of:

- objective friction reduction
- exact persistence/duration
- clinical prevention or treatment
- superiority over petroleum jelly or specialized competitors
- universal user preference
- national market prevalence from a convenience sample
- Korean regulatory claim eligibility
