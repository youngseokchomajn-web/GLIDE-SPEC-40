# Brand Research Gate Closure Plan — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## Objective

Close the remaining evidence gates before the first-product specification is locked. The rule is to separate:

- user report
- market signal
- measured technical performance
- regulatory claim

No one category may be substituted for another.

## Recommended study sequence

### Phase A — User evidence

1. Recruit Korean runners across 10K, half, marathon, 50K and 100K+.
2. Record body area, garment/equipment, temperature, humidity/water, application timing, amount, first symptom, failure time, reapplication, substitute behavior and spend.
3. Include both users with no problem and users with recurrent failure.
4. Keep male/female and relevant anatomical interfaces explicit rather than pooling them.

### Phase B — Competitive benchmark

At minimum benchmark petroleum jelly and leading specialized anti-chafe formats. Match:
- application amount per unit area
- body area
- textile
- moisture/sweat condition
- mechanical cycles
- temperature
- test duration

Outputs should include friction, persistence, migration, transfer, sensory/application and reapplication burden.

### Phase C — Prototype UX

Test multiple formats because format itself may affect performance:
- stick
- balm/ointment
- roll-on
- wipe/single-use

Test representative sites including inner thigh, groin/waistband, under-bra/sports-bra interface, underarm, nipple area and equipment contact zones where appropriate and safe.

### Phase D — Skin compatibility and recovery

Treat prevention/protection and post-chafe recovery as different jobs. Do not use a successful anti-friction result as evidence for recovery efficacy.

### Phase E — Regulatory/claim mapping

Before public claims are written, map each claim to:
1. intended cosmetic function
2. Korean regulatory classification
3. required safety/quality evidence
4. exact substantiation source
5. wording allowed in Korean market materials

## Current evidence interpretation

Korean runner evidence now includes reports of sweat-related Vaseline wash-off, clothing-motion interaction and perceived duration limits. These are hypothesis-generating only. International sports-dermatology literature likewise supports treating friction as a skin–textile/equipment/environment interaction rather than a single-product problem. cite-source-placeholder

A 2026 endurance-athlete skin-barrier review identifies prolonged repetitive loading, sweat, clothing/equipment occlusion, thermal and water exposure as interacting stressors. A 2026 review focused on female athletes specifically describes bra-related friction as influenced by duration, fit, textile, climate and chest-strap interactions. These support the test architecture but do not prove any particular formulation will work. 

## Decision rule

Do not lock:
- final formula
- final format
- numerical duration claim
- price
- final first-product SKU

until the relevant gates above have evidence.

## What success looks like

The first product should emerge from a validated failure scenario with a measurable advantage, not from a preferred ingredient, a fashionable format, or a generic anti-chafe category label.
