## Original purpose

Before launching the full Korean runner survey, conduct a small 5–10 person qualitative pilot to verify that the research instrument actually separates high-value failure scenarios rather than merely measuring general anti-chafe awareness or product preference.

This is a customer/problem discovery exercise. It is not a product efficacy test.

Reactivate only if one or more of the following occurs:

3. A Korean/local usage behavior cannot be resolved from existing public evidence.
4. Direct qualitative evidence is needed to interpret a specific high-value failure scenario.

## Core question

> 어떤 러닝 상황에서 기존 피부 마찰 해결책이 더 이상 충분하지 않다고 느끼며, 그 순간 다른 해결책으로 바꿀 만큼 가치가 큰가?

## What the pilot must discover

1. The concrete failure moment: where, when, under what conditions.
2. The actual mechanism perceived by the runner: friction, sweat, garment movement, seam, equipment, migration, sensory burden, etc.
3. What solution was being used immediately before failure.
4. What the runner did next and whether that behavior actually changed the outcome.
5. Whether the problem is recurrent or a one-off event.
6. Whether the runner has already switched products or methods.
7. Whether switching was driven by performance, usability, sensory experience, price, or another factor.
8. Which scenario appears important enough to support a dedicated product concept.

## Recruitment

Target: 5–10 Korean runners.

Seek variation rather than statistical representativeness:
- 10K/half/marathon/ultra experience
- short vs long typical run duration
- road/trail
- male/female/relevant anatomy
- low/moderate/heavy sweating
- recurrent vs occasional chafing
- users of petroleum jelly, specialized anti-chafe, tape, clothing/equipment solutions, and no solution

Do not treat pilot percentages as population prevalence.

## Original interview flow

1. Recent event reconstruction
2. Failure moment
3. Current workaround
4. Switching trigger
5. Neutral concept reaction

The original interview was designed to code:

`USER SEGMENT × BODY AREA × TIME × MOTION × ENVIRONMENT × CLOTHING/EQUIPMENT × CURRENT SOLUTION × FAILURE MOMENT × RESPONSE × SWITCHING TRIGGER`

## Original evidence boundary

Pilot interviews do not establish prevalence, efficacy, superiority, clinical benefit, regulatory eligibility, or national market size.
