# Public Web Evidence — Round 11

Date: 2026-09-13

## Purpose

Final discrimination pass for the current beachhead question:

> Is S1 primarily a clothing/fit problem that should be solved by apparel, or is there a persistent product-level problem worth solving with an endurance skin product?

This pass is not a product test and does not establish efficacy, prevalence, WTP, or superiority.

## Evidence reviewed

### 1. Long-run failure can persist despite changing clothing

A September 2026 REI long-run account describes a runner whose thigh burning begins around 10 miles despite trying different shorts and petroleum jelly. The editorial conclusion is that long-run chafing often requires a combination of strategies rather than a single intervention. This is useful because it directly documents a case where both apparel and topical intervention had already been attempted without a complete solution.

Source: REI, "Ask an Outsider: What's the Best Way to Prevent Chafing on Long Runs and Rides?", 2026-09-10.

### 2. Apparel is a legitimate competing solution

REI's current guidance explicitly identifies fit, seams, fabric, snug compression clothing, and lubricant as alternative or complementary interventions for thigh/groin chafing. This confirms that apparel is not merely a secondary consideration; it is a direct part of the solution system.

Source: REI, "Managing Chafing: Prevention & Treatment".

### 3. Apparel alone does not eliminate the category need

A July 2026 community case reports chafing beyond approximately 10 km, with users reporting different solutions including 2-in-1 shorts, longer inseams, compression shorts, tights, Vaseline, Body Glide and other products. One user reports that tights solve the thigh problem but topical protection is still needed for the groin. Another reports that a seam itself created a new chafing location.

Source: r/runninglifestyle, "Long run chafing", 2026-07-20.

### 4. Commercial apparel guidance still pairs clothing with topical protection

A September 2026 marathon apparel guide states that sweat + motion + very high step counts can overwhelm otherwise comfortable fabrics and recommends anti-chafe stick alongside appropriate apparel. This is commercial content and should not be treated as independent efficacy evidence, but it is useful for competitive/category mapping: apparel brands themselves acknowledge topical protection as part of the endurance solution stack.

Source: OFFLINE by Aerie, "Marathon Outfit Guide", 2026-09-10.

### 5. Female interface remains a separate system problem

The 2026 EADV sports-bra chafing guidance identifies long duration, poorly fitted/adapted bras, abrasive textiles, sweating/climate and chest straps as interacting causes. It recommends both fit correction and an additional barrier/anti-chafing cream. This strongly supports the system model and argues against treating S2 as merely a cosmetic problem or merely an apparel problem.

Source: European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology, "Bra-related chafing in female athletes", 2026.

### 6. Current running media treats shorts and compression as anti-chafe design variables

2026 running-gear coverage continues to evaluate inseam, liner design, fit and compression partly through their effect on chafing. This confirms strong substitute pressure from apparel and means a first product cannot simply claim to solve a problem that good apparel already solves.

Sources: Runner's World 2026 running-shorts coverage; 2026 compression-short coverage.

## Discrimination result

### Hypothesis A — "S1 is only an apparel problem"

**Not supported.**

Reason:
- users can experience failure despite changing shorts;
- seams/fit can create new failure points;
- some users combine apparel and topical protection rather than choosing one;
- long-duration conditions can change the failure state during the activity;
- topical protection remains part of expert and commercial prevention guidance.

### Hypothesis B — "S1 is purely a topical-product problem"

**Also not supported.**

Reason:
- apparel geometry, fit, seams and compression materially alter the outcome;
- clothing can solve some users' thigh problems completely;
- a product that ignores garment interaction risks being technically correct but commercially irrelevant.

### Best-supported model

**S1 is a skin–garment interface problem in which topical protection is one controllable component.**

The opportunity is therefore not:

> make a stronger anti-chafe product.

It is:

> design a skin-protection product around the conditions created by prolonged running, including sweat/moisture, repeated motion, garment contact and changing conditions over time.

## Consequence for product concept

The first product should be defined around a **failure situation**, not around an ingredient, format, or generic claim.

Current strongest formulation:

> **Long-duration running skin protection for situations where repeated motion, sweat/moisture and garment contact make ordinary skin-protection routines insufficient or inconvenient.**

S1 remains the leading beachhead, but its anatomical expression should not yet be permanently fixed to inner thigh/groin. The product concept should remain capable of addressing adjacent high-friction interfaces if technical and commercial evidence later supports them.

## What this pass changes

1. S1 survives the strongest current falsification attempt.
2. Apparel remains a major direct substitute and must be included in competitive analysis.
3. The product opportunity is a **system-designed skin protection layer**, not "better Vaseline" or "longer-lasting anti-chafe."
4. S2 remains a viable differentiated expansion/sub-scenario, but current public evidence does not justify replacing S1 as the first beachhead.
5. S3 remains a UX/operational requirement rather than the core product concept.
6. Further generic community collection is unlikely to materially improve the concept decision unless it reveals a stronger S4 scenario or a contradiction to the system model.

## Remaining uncertainty

Public web/community evidence still cannot establish:

- representative Korean prevalence;
- market size;
- WTP;
- objective product performance;
- exact persistence duration;
- superiority versus specific incumbents;
- final formula or format.

These are downstream validation or commercial questions, not reasons to delay concept definition indefinitely.

## Current research status

**Beachhead decision readiness: HIGH, with S1 leading.**

The next step can move from broad discovery into a formal product-concept decision record. Further web research should be triggered only by a specific falsification question, not by a desire to increase the number of examples.
