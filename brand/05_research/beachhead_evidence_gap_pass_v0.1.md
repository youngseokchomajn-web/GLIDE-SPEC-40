# Beachhead Evidence Gap Pass — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## Purpose

Final evidence-gap pass before Korean primary research.

This document does **not** select the final first product. It determines what the existing community/public-web evidence can already support for S1/S2/S3 and what must still be answered locally.

## Scenarios

- **S1:** Long-duration inner-thigh/groin protection under high sweat + garment motion
- **S2:** Long-duration sports-bra/under-breast/waistband interface under heat/humidity
- **S3:** Long-duration protection where initial application works but maintenance/reapplication becomes the failure

## External/public-web cross-check

Current commercial evidence confirms that the broad anti-friction category is already populated and that incumbents explicitly target several of the same use contexts. Body Glide currently positions products around thighs, underarms, sports bras/compression gear, waistbands and gear zones, including sweat/heat/humidity contexts. This means GLIDE-SPEC 40 should not define the opportunity as simply "anti-chafe for runners." [Body Glide Skin Glide, accessed 2026-09-13]

Recent products also explicitly market endurance, heat, sweat, rain, long-distance use and portable/reapplication-oriented formats. Therefore those variables are opportunity inputs, but none is by itself a defensible unmet need. [Run Slick; Friction Prescription; Oltra Glide; Slick Moves, accessed 2026-09-13]

## S1 assessment

### What is already sufficiently supported

- Repetitive inner-thigh/groin friction is a recurring endurance-running problem.
- Sweat/moisture and garment movement can interact with the failure.
- Existing topical products, clothing/fit and compression are all part of the solution set.
- Users can switch between topical and non-topical solutions.
- S1 is broad enough to support a first product while remaining tied to a concrete use situation.

### What remains unresolved

- Whether S1 is the highest-value recurring scenario among Korean endurance runners.
- Whether Korean runners solve S1 primarily with topical products, clothing/fit, compression or combinations.
- Whether S1 generates enough actual switching to justify a new product.
- Whether the economic burden is meaningful enough to support commercial entry.

### Judgment

**Strongest current beachhead candidate, but not selected yet.**

## S2 assessment

### What is already sufficiently supported

- Sports-bra/under-breast/band/strap interfaces are distinct friction environments.
- Women use topical products, tape and clothing/fit changes as competing solutions.
- The problem can be highly consequential and anatomically specific.
- The scenario has stronger differentiation potential than a generic anti-chafe proposition.

### What remains unresolved

- Korean target-segment size within the intended endurance population.
- Frequency and recurrence relative to S1.
- Actual switching behavior and current workaround entrenchment.
- Willingness to pay for a dedicated solution.

### Judgment

**High-value alternative; potentially more differentiated than S1, but commercial localization is insufficient.**

## S3 assessment

### What is already sufficiently supported

- Reapplication occurs in long-duration use.
- Carryability, reachability and interruption burden affect the practical solution.
- Portable formats can be valued by endurance users.

### What remains unresolved

- Whether reapplication is the primary failure or merely a consequence of another failure.
- Which anatomical/environmental scenario creates the reapplication burden.
- Whether users actually switch products because of reapplication burden.

### Judgment

**Important cross-cutting UX variable, but currently too format/operation-centric to be the first product concept by itself.**

| Korean localization required | High | Very high | High |

Scores are qualitative evidence judgments, not prevalence or market-share estimates.

## What the Korean study must decide

The Korean study should answer only the following decision questions:

1. Among S1/S2/S3, which scenario has the strongest combination of **intensity × recurrence**?
2. Which scenario has the clearest **existing-solution failure**?
3. Which scenario produces **actual switching**, not only stated interest?
4. Which scenario has meaningful **current spend / workaround cost**?
5. Which scenario remains attractive after including **non-topical substitutes**?
6. Which Korean segment/context materially changes the ranking?
7. Does an unexpected S4 scenario outperform the predefined candidates?

## Questions explicitly deferred

Do not use the Korean concept study to decide:

- exact formula
- ingredient system
- stick/cream/balm/gel format
- exact persistence duration
- superiority versus incumbents
- clinical efficacy
- regulatory eligibility
- final package

Those belong downstream of beachhead selection.

## Current judgment

**S1 remains the leading candidate. S2 remains the principal alternative. S3 remains a cross-cutting operational variable rather than a standalone first-product concept.**

The correct next step is therefore a targeted Korean study that can overturn S1 if S2, S3, or S4 demonstrates materially stronger commercial evidence.

## Evidence boundary

Community and public-web evidence is discovery/hypothesis evidence. Commercial claims, prevalence, WTP, objective performance and superiority require appropriate primary or controlled evidence.
