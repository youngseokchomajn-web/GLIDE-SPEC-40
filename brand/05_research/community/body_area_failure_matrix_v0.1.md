# Body-Area × Distance × Environment × Solution Failure Matrix — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: hypothesis map; not a validated market or performance standard.

## Purpose

Convert individual community reports into testable problem definitions. The unit of analysis is not simply "chafing" but:

**body area × elapsed time/distance × environment × clothing/equipment × current solution × failure mode**

Community reports are signals only. They do not establish causal performance or universal product superiority.

## Key observations

1. **Duration is not enough.** Time interacts with sweat, rain, heat, clothing and body area.
2. **There is no single incumbent winner.** Reports contain strong advocates for Vaseline, Body Glide, SNB, 2Toms and Gold Bond.
3. **Failure can be localized.** A product can work on one body area and fail on another.
4. **Reapplication is a product-system problem.** Carry format, application method, cleanliness and access during an event matter.
5. **Clothing/equipment can be part of the solution.** Some users change liners, compression garments or other equipment rather than changing lubricant.
6. **Cold and heat create opposite format problems.** Balm/stick spreadability and heat softening should be evaluated separately.
7. **The most promising research question is not "which product lasts longest?" but "under what defined conditions does protection become unacceptable, and what intervention prevents that failure?"

## Candidate failure moments

### F1 — Late-race protection collapse
Protection is acceptable initially but becomes inadequate after prolonged repeated motion.

### F2 — Wet-condition failure
Rain, sweat or water exposure changes the interface enough that a previously acceptable product no longer protects adequately.

### F3 — High-sweat-area failure
Axilla, groin, inner thigh or nipple areas behave differently from lower-sweat areas.

### F4 — Body-area mismatch
A product works on thighs but is uncomfortable, migrates, runs, or fails in folds/creases.

### F5 — Reapplication friction
The product may work, but carrying and applying it during a race is inconvenient, messy, inaccessible, or unhygienic.

### F6 — Climate-format conflict
A formulation that is pleasant/spreadable in one temperature range becomes too soft in heat or too hard in cold.

### F7 — Clothing/equipment interaction
Garment choice, seams, compression, waistbands, hydration packs or other equipment changes the friction system enough to alter outcome.

## Evidence boundary

These failure moments are hypotheses generated from community reports. They are **not yet product requirements**. Before GLIDE-SPEC 40 converts any of them into numerical targets, each must pass:

`community signal → repeated independent evidence → mechanism hypothesis → controlled test method → comparative data → product requirement`
