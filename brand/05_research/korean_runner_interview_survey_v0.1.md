# Korean Runner Interview & Survey Protocol — v0.2

Date: 2026-09-13

## Objective

Convert the existing Korean runner evidence into structured prevalence, failure-context, behavior, switching, and willingness-to-pay evidence before first-product specification lock.

This is research design, not a product claim.

## Update from v0.1

This revision incorporates Community Evidence Round 5. The survey now treats the primary failure unit as a system interaction:

`skin × product × garment × equipment × motion × sweat/water × time`

The protocol also separates first symptom onset from unacceptable protection/failure, captures garment migration and equipment-induced micro-motion, records non-topical substitutes, and adds sensory/cleanup and reachability variables as explicit switching factors.

## Target sample

### Quantitative survey

Target: 100–200 qualified Korean runners for directional segmentation.

Minimum screening:
- ran at least 10 km in the last 3 months
- has run at least one 10K, half marathon, marathon, trail/ultra, or equivalent long-duration session
- has experienced or intentionally prevented skin friction/chafing during exercise OR has used a topical/tape/garment/equipment solution for it

Do not pool all runners into one group. Preserve:
- 10K / half / marathon / 50K / 100K+
- road / trail
- male / female / relevant anatomical interface
- hot-humid / hot-dry / cool-dry / rain-wet / cold exposure
- recurrent problem / occasional problem / no meaningful problem

### Qualitative interviews

Target: 20–30 runners selected to maximize variation, not convenience.

Prioritize:
- repeated failures despite current products
- long-distance runners who reapply during activity
- users who switched between petroleum jelly, specialized anti-chafe, tape, clothing, or equipment changes
- sports-bra/under-bra/waistband/hydration-vest friction cases
- sensitive-skin or product-intolerance cases
- users who report no problem despite long-duration activity
- cases where clothing/equipment, rather than a topical product, solved the problem

## Survey modules

### A. Running profile

1. Typical weekly running distance
2. Longest recent run
3. Typical longest-run duration
4. Road/trail/indoor mix
5. Typical climate and season
6. Typical clothing and equipment at longest-run exposure

### B. Problem prevalence

For each body area, ask whether the respondent experienced friction/chafing in the last 12 months:
- inner thigh
- groin/waistband
- buttock/gluteal area
- nipple
- underarm
- under-bra/sports-bra contact
- hydration vest/pack contact
- foot/toe/shoe interface
- other

Record frequency separately: never / once / occasional / frequent / almost every long run.

Also record the dominant interface where known:
- skin-on-skin
- skin-on-garment
- skin-on-equipment
- mixed/unclear

### C. Failure event

For the most recent meaningful event, record separately:
- distance/time when symptoms first appeared
- distance/time when protection became unacceptable or the runner changed strategy
- distance/time when the problem was fully controlled, if known
- temperature estimate/category
- rain/water/sweat/humidity condition
- clothing/equipment involved
- whether clothing migrated, rolled, bunched, or exposed a previously protected area
- whether a seam, edge, strap, waistband, bra band, vest/pack, or other equipment contact point was involved
- product or intervention used
- application timing
- approximate amount if known
- application area/reachability
- reapplication opportunity
- whether reapplication was attempted
- what ultimately solved the problem

Failure taxonomy:
- friction breakthrough
- migration/exposure of skin contact
- sweat dilution/wash-off
- water exposure
- heat softening/melting
- cold hardening/brittleness
- residue/grease/wet feel
- clothing transfer/staining
- odor/sensory issue
- application difficulty
- reachability problem
- reapplication difficulty
- skin irritation/compatibility
- garment/equipment interaction
- solution limited by clothing fit or construction

### D. Current solution and switching

Ask respondents to select all used:
- petroleum jelly
- Body Glide
- Squirrel's Nut Butter
- Chamois Butt'r
- other anti-chafe product
- tape/bandage/adhesive barrier
- clothing change
- compression/fit change
- longer liner/2-in-1 or other apparel change
- equipment/pack adjustment or replacement
- no intervention

For the primary solution ask:
- why chosen
- what works well
- what is annoying
- when it fails
- whether it requires reapplication
- whether they carry it
- whether they can reach the affected area while running
- why they have not switched
- what alternative they tried before/after it
- whether the final solution was topical, adhesive, clothing, equipment, or a combination

Explicitly distinguish:
1. product failure
2. clothing/fit failure
3. equipment interaction
4. application/reapplication failure
5. skin compatibility problem
6. sensory/cleanup objection

Do not assume the topical product was the root cause when the respondent reports a clothing or equipment change as the effective solution.

### E. Product priorities

Ask respondents to rate the importance of:
- friction reduction
- persistence
- sweat resistance
- water resistance
- heat/cold usability
- low residue
- no clothing transfer
- low odor / acceptable sensory profile
- easy application
- easy reachability
- easy reapplication
- skin compatibility
- compact carry
- price/value

Add a forced-choice exercise where feasible: select the top 3 priorities rather than only rating every attribute highly.

Do not treat stated importance as proof of market demand; compare with observed behavior, switching, and purchase history.

### F. Price/value

First record actual behavior:
- current product name
- purchase price if known
- package size
- approximate purchases per year
- approximate annual spend
- alternative annual spend on tape/clothing/equipment used for the same problem
- willingness to switch at equal performance

Then use a Van Westendorp-style four-question battery after showing a standardized product concept. The four thresholds are: too cheap to trust, good-value/bargain, getting expensive, and too expensive to consider. This method is useful for an early directional acceptable-price range but does not establish actual demand or profit-maximizing price.

Add a purchase-intent check at selected price points because direct price questions alone can overstate actual willingness to buy.

### G. Trade-off / concept choice

After the baseline questions, present 4–6 randomized product concepts varying:
- protection persistence
- sweat/water resistance
- format
- skin compatibility positioning
- carry/reapplication convenience
- sensory/transfer profile
- price

Include at least one non-topical or keep-current option when the research design permits. This prevents the exercise from assuming that every runner wants a new topical product.

Ask respondents to choose one rather than rating every feature independently. The purpose is to observe trade-offs rather than collect a list of universally desirable features.

## Interview guide

### Opening

“최근 장거리 러닝에서 피부가 불편했던 순간을 하나만 구체적으로 설명해 주세요.”

Then reconstruct the event chronologically before naming or evaluating products.

### Critical probes

- 정확히 몇 km/몇 시간쯤이었나?
- 증상이 처음 나타난 시점과 더 이상 버티기 어려워진 시점은 각각 언제였나?
- 어느 부위였나?
- 피부끼리였나, 옷과 피부였나, 장비와 피부였나?
- 당시 옷과 장비는 무엇이었나?
- 옷이 말리거나 내려가거나 뭉치거나 움직였나?
- 솔기, 밴드, 브라, 스트랩, 베스트/팩 같은 특정 접점이 있었나?
- 땀/비/습도/추위는 어땠나?
- 무엇을 언제 얼마나 발랐나?
- 바르기 어려운 부위였나?
- 처음 문제가 생긴 시점은?
- 제품이 실패했다고 느낀 순간은?
- 다시 발랐나? 다시 바를 기회가 있었나?
- 가지고 뛰었나?
- 제품 대신 옷/테이프/장비를 바꿨나?
- 결국 무엇이 문제를 해결했나?
- 왜 그 해결책을 선택했나?
- 돈을 더 내고 해결하고 싶은 문제였나?
- 현재 제품을 계속 쓰는 이유는 무엇인가?
- 무엇이 바뀌면 제품을 바꿀 것인가?

Do not ask leading questions such as “바세린이 오래 못 가지 않나요?” Ask for the event first and classify the reported failure afterward.

## Analysis plan

### Primary outputs

1. body-area × distance/time prevalence matrix
2. interface × failure-mode matrix (skin-skin / skin-garment / skin-equipment)
3. condition × failure-mode matrix
4. clothing/equipment migration and micro-motion patterns
5. current-solution switching matrix
6. topical vs adhesive vs apparel vs equipment substitution matrix
7. reapplication/carry/reachability behavior
8. sensory/cleanup objection patterns
9. annual category spend, including relevant non-topical substitutes
10. acceptable-price distribution
11. price × segment differences
12. willingness-to-switch drivers

### Segmentation

At minimum compare:
- marathon/ultra vs shorter-distance runners
- recurrent vs occasional/no problem
- male vs female/relevant anatomy
- road vs trail
- warm/humid exposure vs other climates
- topical-primary vs non-topical-primary solution users

Do not infer causality from cross-sectional survey differences.

## Gate criteria

The user-evidence gate can be marked “directionally closed” only when:

- repeated failure patterns appear across multiple independent respondents;
- important body-area/condition/interface cells are not represented by a single anecdote;
- first symptom onset and unacceptable-protection/failure timing are both captured where possible;
- current solution and switching behavior are measured, not inferred;
- clothing/equipment substitutes are explicitly measured;
- price results are reported by runner segment;
- contradictory cases remain visible;
- qualitative interviews explain why the quantitative patterns occur.

Even if these criteria are met, technical performance and regulatory gates remain open.

## Evidence boundary

This study can establish customer/problem prevalence, behavior, preferences, switching patterns, and directional price sensitivity. It cannot establish formulation efficacy, clinical benefit, objective duration, superiority over incumbents, or regulatory claim eligibility.

Community-derived failure taxonomies are hypotheses for structured measurement. They must not be converted into prevalence claims unless supported by the survey sample, and they must not be converted into objective performance claims without controlled testing.
