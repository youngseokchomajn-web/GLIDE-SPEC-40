# Korean Runner Interview & Survey Protocol — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## Objective

Convert the existing Korean runner evidence into structured prevalence, failure-context, behavior, and willingness-to-pay evidence before first-product specification lock.

This is research design, not a product claim.

## Target sample

### Quantitative survey

Target: 100–200 qualified Korean runners for directional segmentation.

Minimum screening:
- ran at least 10 km in the last 3 months
- has run at least one 10K, half marathon, marathon, trail/ultra, or equivalent long-duration session
- has experienced or intentionally prevented skin friction/chafing during exercise OR has used a topical/tape/garment solution for it

Do not pool all runners into one group. Preserve:
- 10K / half / marathon / 50K / 100K+
- road / trail
- male / female / relevant anatomical interface
- hot-humid / hot-dry / cool-dry / rain-wet / cold exposure
- recurrent problem / occasional problem / no meaningful problem

### Qualitative interviews

Target: 20–30 runners selected to maximize variation, not convenience.

Prioritize:
- repeated failures despite current products
- long-distance runners who reapply during activity
- users who switched between petroleum jelly, specialized anti-chafe, tape, clothing, or equipment changes
- sports-bra/under-bra/waistband/hydration-vest friction cases
- sensitive-skin or product-intolerance cases
- users who report no problem despite long-duration activity

## Survey modules

### A. Running profile

1. Typical weekly running distance
2. Longest recent run
3. Typical longest-run duration
4. Road/trail/indoor mix
5. Typical climate and season

### B. Problem prevalence

For each body area, ask whether the respondent experienced friction/chafing in the last 12 months:
- inner thigh
- groin/waistband
- buttock/gluteal area
- nipple
- underarm
- under-bra/sports-bra contact
- hydration vest/pack contact
- foot/toe/shoe interface
- other

Record frequency separately: never / once / occasional / frequent / almost every long run.

### C. Failure event

For the most recent meaningful event, record:
- distance/time when symptoms first appeared
- distance/time when protection became unacceptable
- temperature estimate/category
- rain/water/sweat condition
- clothing/equipment involved
- product or intervention used
- application timing
- approximate amount if known
- reapplication
- what ultimately solved the problem

Failure taxonomy:
- friction breakthrough
- migration
- sweat dilution/wash-off
- water exposure
- heat softening/melting
- cold hardening/brittleness
- residue/grease
- clothing transfer/staining
- odor/sensory issue
- application difficulty
- reapplication difficulty
- skin irritation/compatibility
- garment/equipment interaction

### D. Current solution and switching

Ask respondents to select all used:
- petroleum jelly
- Body Glide
- Squirrel's Nut Butter
- Chamois Butt'r
- other anti-chafe product
- tape/bandage
- clothing change
- compression/fit change
- equipment/pack adjustment
- no intervention

For the primary solution ask:
- why chosen
- what works well
- what is annoying
- when it fails
- whether they reapply
- whether they carry it
- why they have not switched

### E. Product priorities

Ask respondents to rate the importance of:
- friction reduction
- persistence
- sweat resistance
- water resistance
- heat/cold usability
- low residue
- no clothing transfer
- easy application
- easy reachability
- easy reapplication
- skin compatibility
- compact carry
- price/value

Do not treat stated importance as proof of market demand; compare with observed behavior and purchase history.

### F. Price/value

First record actual behavior:
- current product name
- purchase price if known
- package size
- approximate purchases per year
- approximate annual spend
- willingness to switch at equal performance

Then use a Van Westendorp-style four-question battery after showing a standardized product concept. The four thresholds are: too cheap to trust, good-value/bargain, getting expensive, and too expensive to consider. This method is useful for an early directional acceptable-price range but does not establish actual demand or profit-maximizing price. cite-source-placeholder

Add a purchase-intent check at selected price points because direct price questions alone can overstate actual willingness to buy.

### G. Trade-off / concept choice

After the baseline questions, present 4–6 randomized product concepts varying:
- protection persistence
- sweat/water resistance
- format
- skin compatibility positioning
- carry/reapplication convenience
- price

Ask respondents to choose one rather than rating every feature independently. The purpose is to observe trade-offs rather than collect a list of universally desirable features.

## Interview guide

### Opening

“최근 장거리 러닝에서 피부가 불편했던 순간을 하나만 구체적으로 설명해 주세요.”

Then reconstruct the event chronologically.

### Critical probes

- 정확히 몇 km/몇 시간쯤이었나?
- 어느 부위였나?
- 당시 옷과 장비는 무엇이었나?
- 땀/비/습도/추위는 어땠나?
- 무엇을 언제 얼마나 발랐나?
- 처음 문제가 생긴 시점은?
- 제품이 실패했다고 느낀 순간은?
- 다시 발랐나?
- 가지고 뛰었나?
- 제품 대신 옷/테이프/장비를 바꿨나?
- 왜 그 해결책을 선택했나?
- 돈을 더 내고 해결하고 싶은 문제였나?
- 현재 제품을 계속 쓰는 이유는 무엇인가?
- 무엇이 바뀌면 제품을 바꿀 것인가?

Do not ask leading questions such as “바세린이 오래 못 가지 않나요?” Ask for the event first and classify the reported failure afterward.

## Analysis plan

### Primary outputs

1. body-area × distance/time prevalence matrix
2. condition × failure-mode matrix
3. current-solution switching matrix
4. reapplication/carry behavior
5. annual category spend
6. acceptable-price distribution
7. price × segment differences
8. willingness-to-switch drivers

### Segmentation

At minimum compare:
- marathon/ultra vs shorter-distance runners
- recurrent vs occasional/no problem
- male vs female/relevant anatomy
- road vs trail
- warm/humid exposure vs other climates

Do not infer causality from cross-sectional survey differences.

## Gate criteria

The user-evidence gate can be marked “directionally closed” only when:

- repeated failure patterns appear across multiple independent respondents;
- important body-area/condition cells are not represented by a single anecdote;
- current solution and switching behavior are measured, not inferred;
- price results are reported by runner segment;
- contradictory cases remain visible;
- qualitative interviews explain why the quantitative patterns occur.

Even if these criteria are met, technical performance and regulatory gates remain open.

## Evidence boundary

This study can establish customer/problem prevalence, behavior, preferences, and directional price sensitivity. It cannot establish formulation efficacy, clinical benefit, objective duration, superiority over incumbents, or regulatory claim eligibility.
