## Major things that must not be skipped

### 1. Negative evidence
Do not collect only successful product stories. A failure report may identify the exact boundary condition worth solving.

### 2. Contradictions
Do not average contradictory experiences into a generic score. Preserve both and look for the condition that explains the difference.

### 3. Non-product solutions
Compression shorts, liners, clothing construction, tape, powders and behavior changes can solve or alter the same problem. A new cosmetic is not automatically the correct intervention.

### 4. Body-area specificity
Inner thigh, groin, buttock cleft, nipple, axilla, feet and waistband should not be treated as one friction problem.

### 5. Time versus distance
Two runners covering the same distance may have different exposure durations and conditions. Elapsed time must remain an independent variable.

### 6. Climate and moisture
Heat, humidity, sweat, rain, water crossings and cold can change both skin state and product behavior.

### 7. Application and reapplication
A theoretically superior formula can fail commercially if it is difficult to apply at the start or during a race.

### 8. Skin safety and recovery
Prevention is only one part of the skin journey. Product compatibility, irritation, cleansing and post-exercise recovery need separate evidence.

### 9. Economic behavior
The strongest technical problem is not necessarily the strongest business opportunity. Switching cost, price sensitivity, pack size and willingness to pay remain open.

### 10. Regulatory boundary
Community reports can generate hypotheses but cannot support medical, therapeutic, or quantified superiority claims.

## Decision gate

Do not freeze the first-product specification until the following are available:

- sufficient independent community cases across priority cells
- at least one plausible explanation for major contradictory reports
- customer interviews or equivalent primary research
- defined candidate failure moment(s)
- testable performance hypotheses
- initial manufacturing/formulation feasibility review
- safety and regulatory claim boundaries

## Current conclusion

The research is directionally coherent but **not complete enough to finalize the first formula or numerical performance specification**. The correct next step is deeper evidence collection and primary customer research, followed by controlled testing.
