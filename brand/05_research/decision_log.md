# Brand Decision Log — v0.1

This log preserves the reasoning chain behind strategic decisions. It is intentionally separate from polished brand documents.

## D001 — Do not position as "sports Vaseline"

**Observation:** Runners use petroleum jelly and specialized anti-chafe products for friction protection.

**Reasoning:** Existing solutions already have real users and demonstrated utility. A simple "better Vaseline" position does not create a sufficiently defensible strategic category.

**Decision:** Treat anti-chafe as one product function inside a broader endurance-skin proposition.

**What would change this decision:** Strong evidence that a specific petroleum-jelly limitation represents a large, valuable, technically solvable unmet need.

## D002 — Start with endurance runners

**Observation:** Marathon and ultra-distance activity combines prolonged duration, repetitive motion, sweat/moisture, clothing/equipment contact, and environmental exposure.

**Reasoning:** This creates a concentrated environment in which skin-management problems become highly consequential and observable.

**Decision:** Use marathon/ultra runners as the initial beachhead rather than immediately targeting all sports.

**What would change this decision:** User research showing another sport/scenario has a larger, clearer, or more defensible unmet need.

## D003 — Use TIME × MOTION × ENVIRONMENT as the design lens

**Observation:** Skin problems during endurance activity are affected by elapsed time, repetitive movement, moisture/heat/weather, clothing, and equipment.

**Reasoning:** Conventional cosmetic categories do not adequately describe these interacting conditions.

**Decision:** Use TIME × MOTION × ENVIRONMENT as the first strategic abstraction for product discovery.

**What would change this decision:** Research showing a different variable set better predicts user failure or product performance.

## D004 — Treat community reports as evidence, not truth

**Observation:** Athlete communities contain detailed reports about product feel, persistence, failure, reapplication, body area, and conditions. Reports often contradict each other.

**Reasoning:** Contradictions may reflect different distances, environments, anatomy, application amounts, or product formats. Discarding them loses important information.

**Decision:** Preserve individual reports and their context in a community evidence registry. Use repeated patterns to generate hypotheses and controlled-test questions, not unsupported universal claims.

**What would change this decision:** A more rigorous evidence source becomes available for a given question.

## D005 — Search for the failure moment, not the generic problem

**Observation:** "Chafing" is too broad. The useful commercial question is when, where, and under what conditions the current solution stops being acceptable.

**Reasoning:** A precise failure moment can become a measurable product requirement and a focused first-product concept.

**Decision:** Map pain points by body area × distance × elapsed time × environment × existing solution.

**What would change this decision:** Research demonstrates that the dominant unmet need is not a failure moment but another factor such as application UX, residue, recovery, or prevention behavior.

## D006 — Do not assume duration superiority

**Observation:** Community reports about product longevity are mixed.

**Reasoning:** Some users report long persistence; others reapply or switch products under different conditions.

**Decision:** Duration is a research variable, not an established competitor weakness. Any superiority claim must be tested.

**What would change this decision:** Controlled comparative testing establishes a reproducible performance difference.

## D007 — Brand and GLIDE-SPEC 40 remain linked but distinct

**Observation:** Brand strategy defines customer/problem/product intent; GLIDE-SPEC 40 handles technical formulation and validation.

**Reasoning:** Mixing these layers makes it difficult to tell whether a decision came from market evidence or technical evidence.

**Decision:** Keep brand planning under `brand/` while explicitly mapping downstream implications to GLIDE-SPEC 40.

**What would change this decision:** Repository scale or team structure eventually requires a separate brand repository.

## D008 — Do not lock the first product before identifying the beachhead failure scenario

**Observation:** Global/public-web evidence now supports several recurring endurance-skin failure contexts, but it does not establish Korean prevalence, switching behavior, WTP, or which scenario has the highest commercial value. The current product options include long-run friction protection, endurance skin film, sweat-resilient protection, reapplication-first format, and female interface protection.

**Reasoning:** Choosing a formula or format before identifying the highest-value failure scenario risks making the technical solution drive the product definition. The first product should be built around a specific user + situation + failure moment, while the broader Endurance Skin concept remains the category architecture.

**Decision:** Use **Long-Run Friction Shield** as the provisional beachhead problem, **Endurance Skin Protection** as the category logic, and **TIME × MOTION × ENVIRONMENT** as the design lens. Do not yet lock the final anatomical cell, format, formula, duration, or performance claim. Use Korean primary research to select the specific beachhead scenario.

**What would change this decision:** Korean runner research identifies another recurring failure scenario with materially stronger problem intensity, switching behavior, economic value, or strategic defensibility.
