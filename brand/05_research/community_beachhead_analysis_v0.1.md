## What the evidence is strong enough to say

### 1. The problem is real, but not product-only

The recurring failure unit is:

`SKIN × PRODUCT × GARMENT × EQUIPMENT × MOTION × SWEAT/WATER × TIME`

A topical product is often only one component of the user's solution. Garment fit, seams, migration, compression, sports-bra geometry, hydration-vest movement, tape and other interventions can determine whether the problem occurs.

### 2. Long duration is a recurring context, not a fixed mileage threshold

Reports span short-duration failures, marathon-distance problems, 50K/50-mile contexts and ultra-distance use. Some users report failure quickly; others report successful use for very long activity. Therefore the commercial concept should not be "X-hour protection" at this stage.

### 3. Reapplication is an operating variable, not automatically the root problem

Recent 2026 evidence includes repeated reports of reapplication during long runs, especially as heat and duration increase. Other users report products lasting for very long events. The contradiction is strategically useful: reapplication is often a response to changing conditions, not sufficient evidence for a reapplication-first product.

### 4. Non-topical substitutes are direct competitors

Tape, bandages, compression shorts, lined shorts, underwear, sports-bra changes and equipment/fit adjustments repeatedly appear as alternatives. A first product must win against the user's total workaround, not only against another anti-chafe brand.

### 5. Sensory and cleanup can trigger switching

Greasy/wet/dry feel, odor, staining, transfer, portability and application reachability can influence whether a user keeps or changes a solution.

### 6. Body area is a major segmentation variable

Inner thigh/groin, intergluteal, nipple/chest, sports-bra/under-breast, waistband and equipment-contact sites can produce different failure mechanisms and different preferred solutions.

## Round 10 discrimination update

Round 10 focused specifically on evidence that could overturn S1.

### S1 strengthened

Recent long-run reports show:

- severe inner-thigh failure after long duration in heavy sweaters;
- failure despite liberal topical application when liner/seam geometry becomes the dominant source of abrasion;
- clothing changes such as half tights/compression shorts repeatedly used as substitutes or complements;
- environmental differences between dry and wet conditions changing product choice.

This strengthens the proposition that the first problem is a **prolonged skin–garment interface protection problem**, not simply a request for a stronger lubricant.

### S2 remains strong but substitute pressure is high

Sports-bra/under-breast/waistband and hydration-vest cases show the same system structure. However, apparel and equipment design can solve these problems directly, increasing substitute pressure.

### S3 is downgraded as a standalone concept

Reapplication is repeatedly observed, but it frequently follows changing conditions and protection loss. Therefore "reapplication-first stick" remains a UX/format hypothesis rather than the primary product concept.

### S4 remains open

Intergluteal, waistband, nipple/chest and other late-stage failures continue to appear. They remain monitored for evidence strong enough to overturn S1.

## Candidate concept adjudication

### A — Long-Run Friction Shield

**Status: LEADING BEACHHEAD CANDIDATE**

Strongest current fit because it integrates long-duration exposure, sweat/water, repeated movement and garment interaction without requiring a premature technology claim.

Critical limitation: it must not become a generic "lasts longer" anti-chafe claim.

### B — Endurance Skin Film

**Status: CATEGORY ARCHITECTURE**

Strongest explanation of the system-level problem, but too broad for the first SKU.

### C — Sweat-Resilient Run Protection

**Status: CORE DESIGN CONDITION**

Sweat/heat/humidity are important failure amplifiers, but not necessarily the complete customer job.

### D — Reapplication-First Endurance Stick

**Status: UX/FORMAT HYPOTHESIS**

Real operational need, but format-first selection remains premature.

### E — Female Endurance Interface Protection

**Status: HIGH-VALUE ALTERNATIVE BEACHHEAD**

Strong differentiation and switching evidence, but apparel/equipment substitutes and the narrower initial segment keep it behind S1 for now.

## Current concept decision

**Do not run a separate discovery pilot merely because one was planned earlier.**

The active sequence is:

1. Treat community/public-web evidence as the discovery layer.
2. Use S1/S2/S3/S4 as competing hypotheses.
3. Continue web research only when a new source can discriminate among them or overturn an assumption.
4. Do not execute Korean primary research automatically.
5. If a specific unresolved commercial/local question cannot be answered from public data, primary research may be considered as a conditional exception.
6. After a beachhead is selected, update `first_product_concept.md`.
7. Only then move into formulation, OEM/ODM, format, packaging and technical validation.

## What still cannot be decided from public/community data

Public/community evidence generally cannot establish:

- representative Korean population prevalence;
- representative WTP or price elasticity;
- objective controlled performance;
- exact persistence duration;
- superiority versus incumbents;
- clinical prevention/treatment;
- regulatory eligibility;
- final formula;
- final format;
- final package.

These are not automatic reasons to run a survey. The current rule is to proceed with public evidence until a specific decision genuinely requires another evidence layer.

## Research stopping rule

Stop adding generic cases when:

- S1 remains the leading scenario across independent sources;
- new evidence mostly repeats already-known failure modes;
- no credible S4 overturning pattern is emerging;
- remaining uncertainty is about technical performance or representative commercial quantities rather than concept definition.

At that point, move to the concept decision rather than collecting evidence indefinitely.

## Boundaries

This analysis does not decide:

- final anatomy;
- ingredient;
- formula;
- stick/balm/cream/gel format;
- exact duration;
- superiority claim;
- clinical efficacy claim;
- package architecture;
- final price.
