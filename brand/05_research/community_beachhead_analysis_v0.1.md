## What the community evidence is strong enough to say

### 1. The problem is real, but not product-only

The recurring failure unit is:

`SKIN × PRODUCT × GARMENT × EQUIPMENT × MOTION × SWEAT/WATER × TIME`

A topical product is often only one component of the user's solution. Garment fit, seams, migration, compression, sports-bra geometry, hydration vest movement, tape and other interventions can determine whether the problem occurs.

### 2. Long duration is a recurring context, not a fixed mileage threshold

Reports span short-duration failures, marathon-distance problems, 50K/50-mile contexts and ultra-distance use. Some users report failure quickly; others report successful use for very long activity. Therefore the commercial concept should not be "X-hour protection" at this stage.

### 3. Reapplication is a normal operating behavior

Community evidence repeatedly describes carrying product, reapplying during long events, or choosing a format that makes reapplication possible. This means the product job is not necessarily "never reapply"; it may be "maintain protection with minimal interruption when conditions change." fileciteturn118file0

### 4. Non-topical substitutes are direct competitors

Tape, bandages, compression shorts, lined shorts, underwear, sports-bra changes and equipment/fit adjustments repeatedly appear as alternatives. A first product must win against the user's total workaround, not only against another anti-chafe brand. fileciteturn121file0turn122file0

### 5. Sensory and cleanup can trigger switching

Greasy/wet/dry feel, odor, staining, transfer, portability and application reachability appear repeatedly. These are not secondary cosmetic details; they influence whether a user keeps or changes a solution. fileciteturn122file0

### 6. Body area is a major segmentation variable

Inner thigh/groin, intergluteal, nipple/chest, sports-bra/under-breast, waistband and equipment-contact sites can produce different failure mechanisms and different preferred solutions. fileciteturn120file0turn121file0

## Candidate concept adjudication

### A — Long-Run Friction Shield

**Community evidence:** strongest overall fit.

Why:
- repeated long-duration failure contexts;
- persistence + sweat/water + garment interaction recur together;
- switching between topical products is documented;
- reapplication is repeatedly relevant;
- the problem is understandable without requiring a novel technology claim.

Critical limitation:
- must not become a generic "lasts longer" anti-chafe claim.
- the actual beachhead still needs a narrower failure scenario.

**Status: LEADING BEACHHEAD CANDIDATE**

### B — Endurance Skin Film

**Community evidence:** strongest category architecture.

Why:
- community cases repeatedly show the skin/product/garment/equipment system;
- it explains why product-only ranking fails;
- it supports future expansion beyond one anatomical site.

Critical limitation:
- too broad for the first SKU.

**Status: CATEGORY ARCHITECTURE, NOT FIRST-SKU BEACHHEAD**

### C — Sweat-Resilient Run Protection

**Community evidence:** strong environmental condition, but weaker as the sole concept.

Why:
- sweat, humidity, rain and wet clothing repeatedly appear as failure amplifiers;
- Korean cases include explicit sweat wash-off reports.

Critical limitation:
- sweat is usually an interacting condition rather than the complete job;
- a standalone "sweat-resistant" concept risks collapsing into one performance claim.

**Status: CORE DESIGN CONDITION WITHIN A, NOT PRIMARY CATEGORY**

### D — Reapplication-First Endurance Stick

**Community evidence:** real need, but format-first.

Why:
- reapplication and carryability are repeatedly discussed;
- small sticks/portable formats can solve operational friction.

Critical limitation:
- evidence does not justify choosing a stick before the beachhead problem is fixed;
- reachability and application may favor different formats by anatomy.

**Status: UX/FORMAT HYPOTHESIS, NOT CONCEPT LEADER**

### E — Female Endurance Interface Protection

**Community evidence:** potentially the strongest differentiated sub-scenario.

Why:
- sports-bra/under-breast/band/vest interactions recur;
- several cases show topical products losing to tape or garment solutions;
- the interface problem is specific and strategically defensible.

Critical limitation:
- current evidence does not establish that this should be the first commercial beachhead rather than a major product cell/segment.
- Korean prevalence and willingness to pay remain unresolved.

**Status: HIGH-VALUE ALTERNATIVE BEACHHEAD; REQUIRES COMMERCIAL LOCALIZATION EVIDENCE**

## Beachhead scenario candidates generated from community evidence

### S1 — Long-duration inner-thigh/groin protection under high sweat + garment motion

Evidence pattern:
- repeated inner-thigh/groin failures;
- sweat/wash-off reports;
- shorts/tights migration and seams;
- product switching and clothing substitution.

Why it is attractive:
- broad enough to support a first product;
- specific enough to define a real use situation;
- directly aligned with A + C + B.

Main unresolved issue:
- whether Korean runners experience this frequently enough and whether the problem is more valuable than competing apparel solutions.

### S2 — Long-duration sports-bra/under-breast/waistband interface under heat/humidity

Evidence pattern:
- repeated female-specific failures;
- topical-to-tape switching;
- sports-bra geometry and vest interaction;
- high-shear interface.

Why it is attractive:
- highly differentiated;
- strong evidence of switching pressure;
- clear system-level problem.

Main unresolved issue:
- Korean segment size and initial commercial focus.

### S3 — Long-duration protection where initial application works but maintenance/reapplication becomes the failure

Evidence pattern:
- ultra/event reapplication;
- carryability and reachability;
- changing conditions over time;
- portable formats valued.

Why it is attractive:
- clear operational problem.

Main unresolved issue:
- format should not be selected before the underlying failure scenario is confirmed.

## Current concept decision

**Do not run a separate 5–10 person qualitative pilot yet.**

Instead:

1. Treat community evidence as the discovery pilot.
2. Use S1/S2/S3 as the three principal beachhead hypotheses.
3. Perform a final evidence-gap pass against the existing community/public-web corpus.
4. Only send unresolved commercial questions to Korean primary research.
5. Use the Korean study to choose between the scenario hypotheses, not to rediscover generic anti-chafe problems.
6. After selection, update `first_product_concept.md` to the chosen beachhead.
7. Only then move into formulation, OEM/ODM, format, packaging and technical validation.

## What still cannot be decided from community data

Community evidence cannot establish:

- Korean population prevalence
- representative segment size
- WTP or actual price elasticity
- objective performance
- exact persistence duration
- superiority versus incumbents
- clinical prevention/treatment
- regulatory eligibility
- final formula
- final format
- final package

## Research consequence

The next research task is **not more generic community collection**.

It is a **gap analysis**:

`COMMUNITY EVIDENCE → WHAT IS ALREADY ANSWERED → WHAT IS CONTRADICTORY → WHAT IS COMMERCIAL/LOCAL ONLY → MINIMUM PRIMARY RESEARCH`

The goal is to minimize primary research while preserving enough evidence to make a defensible first-product decision.
