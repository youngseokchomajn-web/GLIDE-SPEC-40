# Public Web Pattern Matrix v0.1 — 2026-09

## Purpose

Aggregate public-web evidence into conditional patterns without treating community reports as prevalence or efficacy measurements.

## Pattern matrix

| Variable | Repeated public-web signal | Contradiction / uncertainty | Validation need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time/duration | Longer events can expose failure after earlier success | Some users report Vaseline/Body Glide works for very long events | Controlled time-to-failure under matched conditions |
| Sweat/water | Heavy sweat is repeatedly associated with perceived wash-off/failure | Some users report adequate protection despite heavy sweat | Sweat/water persistence test |
| Heat/humidity | Hot/humid runs are repeatedly described as higher-risk contexts | No standardized threshold | Controlled temperature/humidity matrix |
| Garment fit | Loose/riding garments and fit changes repeatedly implicated | Some failures persist despite apparently good fit | Garment-motion test |
| Seams | Seams can create focal new friction | Seam effects are body-area dependent | Textile/skin friction test |
| Compression/long inseam | Often reported as effective substitute or complement | Can introduce heat/fit trade-offs | Matched garment comparison |
| Sports bra | Distinct bra-band/under-breast/chest failure cell | Some users solve with fit; others need topical/tape | Body-area + garment-motion validation |
| Hydration vest/pack | Equipment can alter garment movement and create localized back friction | Causal mechanism often inferred by user | Equipment interaction test |
| Tape/adhesive | Often adopted after topical failure or for high-shear sites | Adhesive can cause skin reactions | Adhesive compatibility + durability screening |
| Reapplication | Common long-event behavior | Frequency is not quantifiable from anecdotes | Reapplication burden study |
| Reachability | Back/other difficult sites constrain application choice | Anatomy and mobility vary | Reachability UX test |
| Sensory | Greasy/dry feel, odor, cleanup influence switching | Subjective and product-specific | Standardized sensory/cleanup panel |
| Textile transfer | Staining concerns can drive switching | Reports are not material tests | Transfer/staining bench test |
| Carryability | Small sticks are valued for race carry | Tube size and aid-station logistics vary | Carry/reapplication UX test |
| Product ranking | No universal winner; preferences conflict | Strong selection bias in community reports | Blind/matched incumbent comparison |

## Current highest-value hypotheses

1. The primary competitive unit is not an anti-chafe ingredient but a **skin–clothing–equipment–environment system intervention**.
2. The strongest potential product differentiation may come from combining persistence with low transfer, acceptable sensory feel, easy application, and feasible reapplication.
3. Certain high-shear or difficult-to-reach sites may require different formats or a complementary system rather than one universal formula.
4. Apparel and adhesive interventions must remain explicit alternatives in product concept testing.

## What public data can and cannot answer

### Can inform
- failure-mode taxonomy
- candidate body-area cells
- candidate environmental cells
- switching reasons
- substitute categories
- UX requirements
- technical-test hypotheses

### Cannot reliably establish alone
- population prevalence
- representative market shares
- objective efficacy
- exact duration
- causal superiority
- clinical benefit
- final willingness-to-pay

## Next research action

Continue targeted public-web collection until major failure cells are saturated, while tracking source overlap and contradictions. Only then use a small direct survey/interview layer to close questions that public evidence cannot answer.
