# Korean Runner Study Execution Plan — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## Purpose

This document converts `korean_runner_survey_field_instrument_v0.2.md` into a field-execution plan.

The study is **not a national prevalence survey** and is not intended to prove product efficacy. Its purpose is to discriminate among the current beachhead hypotheses S1/S2/S3 while preserving S4 for an unexpected scenario.

## Decision question

> Among Korean endurance runners, which recurring skin-friction scenario has the strongest combination of problem intensity, recurrence, existing-solution gap, actual switching, economic relevance, solvability, and differentiation to become the first commercial product beachhead?

## 1. Recommended sample

### Preferred

**N = 100–120 qualified respondents**.

Reason: enough observations to compare S1/S2/S3 without pretending to produce population prevalence estimates.

### Minimum viable

**N = 60–80 qualified respondents** if recruitment cost or access is constrained.

Below 60, treat the study as directional qualitative/behavioral evidence and avoid strong scenario-ranking claims unless the differences are large and the qualitative evidence is consistent.

## 2. Recruitment structure

Use purposive recruitment rather than a general population panel.

Target respondents should have recent endurance-running experience, with deliberate coverage of:

- 21.1 km+ runners;
- marathon-distance runners;
- 42.2 km+ / ultra-exposed runners;
- heavy/very-heavy sweaters;
- hot/humid and rain/wet running exposure;
- road and trail;
- respondents with recurring friction/chafing history;
- women with sports-bra/under-breast/band experience;
- runners who have actually used a topical anti-chafe solution;
- runners who use tape, compression, clothing or equipment changes as alternatives.

Do not quota-fill S1/S2/S3 in advance. The point is to observe which scenario naturally appears among qualified respondents.

## 3. Suggested recruitment mix

For N=100–120, aim approximately for:

- 50–60 respondents with marathon-distance or longer experience;
- 20–30 women with relevant sports-bra/band exposure;
- 20–30 respondents with ultra/50 km+ exposure where available;
- broad representation of road/trail and hot/humid exposure.

These categories overlap. They are recruitment controls, not mutually exclusive strata.

Do not force exact percentages if doing so distorts the real respondent pool.

## 4. Screening rules

Required:

1. age eligible for the recruitment channel;
2. at least one 10 km+ run in the previous 3 months;
3. sufficient recall of a recent meaningful friction/skin-protection event, or an explicit "none" response;
4. consent to anonymous research use.

Exclude from scenario ranking but retain in screening accounting when:

- no qualifying recent running exposure;
- fabricated/inconsistent responses;
- speed-completion or straight-line response patterns;
- cannot distinguish personal experience from advice heard from others.

Never silently delete exclusions. Report screened, eligible, completed, and excluded counts.

## 5. Interview/survey mode

The field instrument can be administered as:

- structured online survey for scale;
- short assisted interview for high-value respondents;
- hybrid collection.

If resources permit, use **10–15 assisted interviews within the larger sample** to preserve richer failure narratives and clarify contradictions. These are not a mandatory discovery pilot; they are a qualitative interpretation layer attached to the same scenario-discrimination study.

## 6. Order effects

Keep the most recent real event before concept cards.

Do not expose S1/S2/S3 labels before the respondent has described their own experience.

Randomize or rotate concept-card order for C1/C2 where technically feasible.

Keep a fixed neutral wording for all cards.

## 7. Primary variables

For every respondent, derive:

### Scenario

`S1 / S2 / S3 / S4 / NONE`

### Problem

- intensity 1–5
- recurrence
- disruption
- failure/insufficiency moment

### Existing-solution gap

- failed topical protection
- sweat/water interaction
- garment/equipment interaction
- reapplication burden
- reachability/carryability
- sensory/cleanup burden
- non-topical workaround superiority

### Switching

- ever switched: yes/no
- from → to
- trigger
- current solution retention

### Economics

- current spend
- annual purchase frequency
- additional workaround spend
- price sensitivity

### Concept response

- relevance
- purchase consideration
- keep-current option

## 8. Scenario scoring

Use a 1–5 score for the following dimensions after data cleaning:

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Problem intensity | How severe/meaningful the problem is to the respondent |
| Recurrence | How repeatedly the scenario occurs |
| Existing-solution gap | How often current solutions become insufficient/unacceptable |
| Actual switching | Evidence of behavior change, not just stated interest |
| Economic relevance | Existing spend and credible willingness to change spend |
| Product solvability | Plausible opportunity for a topical/skin-product intervention |
| Differentiation | Ability to define a specific product problem beyond generic anti-chafe |

Do not mechanically average the seven dimensions into a single score.

Use the scorecard as a comparison aid, then inspect the underlying narratives and contradictions.

## 9. Decision rules

### SELECT

Use only when one scenario clearly dominates on the important behavioral dimensions and has no unresolved contradiction that could materially change the commercial interpretation.

### CONDITIONAL SELECT

Use when one scenario leads but one major uncertainty remains, such as segment concentration, switching, or economics.

### HOLD

Use when two scenarios remain close or evidence quality is insufficient.

### REJECT

Use when a scenario has weak recurrence, weak problem intensity, little switching, or is primarily a format/feature preference rather than a meaningful user problem.

## 10. Critical interpretation rule

A scenario does **not** win because:

- it has the largest raw mention count;
- it has the highest hypothetical purchase intent;
- respondents say it sounds innovative;
- it maps neatly to an existing product format;
- it is easier to formulate.

A scenario wins when the evidence shows a meaningful recurring problem that users currently solve imperfectly and would plausibly change behavior or spending to solve better.

## 11. Contradiction handling

Preserve cases such as:

- user reports no problem despite identical distance;
- Vaseline works extremely well for one runner but is rejected by another;
- clothing solves the issue without topical product;
- S2 is highly painful but very small in the recruited segment;
- frequent reapplication exists but users are satisfied with it;
- respondents express high purchase interest but never switch solutions.

Contradictions are segmentation signals, not noise to be averaged away.

## 12. Required analysis tables

Produce at minimum:

1. respondent profile table;
2. S1/S2/S3/S4 assignment table;
3. scenario × intensity/recurrence matrix;
4. scenario × current solution matrix;
5. scenario × actual switching matrix;
6. scenario × workaround/substitute matrix;
7. scenario × economics matrix;
8. scenario × concept-choice matrix;
9. contradiction register;
10. segment cells that materially change the ranking.

## 13. Segment cells to inspect

At minimum:

- 21.1–42.2 km vs 42.2 km+;
- marathon vs ultra-exposed;
- heavy/very-heavy sweat vs lower sweat;
- hot/humid vs other conditions;
- road vs trail;
- women with sports-bra exposure vs others;
- topical users vs non-topical workaround users;
- actual switchers vs non-switchers.

These are analytical cells, not claims about the Korean running population.

## 14. Stop conditions

Stop primary recruitment when either:

### A. Evidence convergence

One scenario has clear qualitative and behavioral separation and additional respondents are mostly repeating already-observed evidence.

### B. Evidence remains unresolved

The leading scenarios remain close despite sufficient sample size. In this case do not force a product decision. Move to targeted follow-up research or a narrower recruitment cell.

## 15. What happens after the study

### If S1 wins

Update `first_product_concept.md` to a specific S1 beachhead and define the exact failure moment before technical work.

### If S2 wins

Create a female-interface-specific first-product concept and re-evaluate market size, formulation constraints, and communication boundaries.

### If S3 wins

Do not immediately choose a stick. First determine which underlying failure scenario creates the reapplication burden; format follows the problem.

### If S4 wins

Create a new concept option and decision-log entry before formulation work.

## 16. Downstream lock

Until the decision gate is closed, do not lock:

- final anatomy;
- ingredient;
- formula;
- stick/balm/cream/gel format;
- exact duration;
- superiority claim;
- package architecture;
- final price;
- clinical/regulatory claim.

## 17. Evidence boundary

This study can support a **commercial beachhead decision within the recruited sample**.

It cannot by itself establish Korean national prevalence, objective efficacy, clinical prevention, exact persistence, superiority, or regulatory eligibility.
