# Community → Korea Gap Analysis — v0.1

Date: 2026-09-13

## Purpose

Determine which first-product concept questions are already sufficiently answered by the existing community/public-web evidence and which questions genuinely require Korean primary research.

This prevents the Korean study from repeating saturated global/community discovery and keeps the current objective on **product concept selection**, not product testing.

## Decision frame

Principal beachhead hypotheses:

- **S1:** Long-duration inner-thigh/groin protection under high sweat + garment motion
- **S2:** Long-duration sports-bra/under-breast/waistband interface under heat/humidity
- **S3:** Long-duration protection where initial application works but maintenance/reapplication becomes the failure

Strategic architecture remains:

- **A — Long-Run Friction Shield:** first-product problem architecture
- **B — Endurance Skin Film:** long-term category architecture
- **C — Sweat-Resilient Protection:** environmental design condition

## Evidence classification

### G0 — Already answered sufficiently for concept discovery

The existing evidence is sufficient to treat the following as established **hypothesis-level signals**:

1. Chafing/failure is condition-dependent rather than product-only.
2. Time/distance interacts with sweat, water, motion, clothing and equipment.
3. Reapplication is a recurring operating behavior.
4. Garment migration and seams can create or amplify failure.
5. Tape, apparel and equipment changes are direct substitutes.
6. Sports-bra/under-breast/band interfaces deserve a separate scenario cell.
7. Sensory/cleanup variables can cause switching.
8. Carryability and reachability matter during long events.
9. Incumbent products have contradictory user outcomes; no universal winner can be inferred.
10. A generic "anti-chafe" product is not the most defensible conceptual framing.

**Action:** Do not ask the Korean sample to rediscover these as open-ended discovery questions.

### G1 — Local/commercial questions requiring Korean primary research

These remain unresolved because community evidence cannot provide representative Korean estimates:

1. Which of S1/S2/S3 is most common among the target Korean endurance-runner population?
2. Which scenario causes the greatest perceived problem intensity?
3. Which scenario produces actual switching behavior rather than hypothetical interest?
4. Which current workaround is most entrenched in Korea?
5. How much do Korean runners currently spend on topical products, tape, apparel and related workarounds?
6. What additional price would make a runner consider switching?
7. Which concept has the highest neutral purchase intent when compared with "keep current solution"?
8. Are female-interface problems large enough commercially to be the first SKU rather than a dedicated product cell?
9. Which Korean climate/use conditions most strongly change solution choice?
10. Which domestic products/brands/channels are actually used by the target segment?

**Action:** These are legitimate Korean survey targets.

### G2 — Technical questions that should NOT be answered by the Korean concept survey

The following belong downstream:

- exact persistence duration
- objective sweat/water resistance
- coefficient of friction or tribology
- film retention
- temperature-dependent rheology
- skin compatibility
- transfer/staining measurement
- superiority versus incumbents
- exact formula or ingredient choice
- final stick/balm/cream format
- regulatory claim eligibility

**Action:** Preserve as technical validation hypotheses after beachhead selection.

## What the Korean study should actually measure

### Priority 1 — Scenario selection

For each respondent, identify the most recent meaningful event and code:

`DISTANCE × TIME × WEATHER × SWEAT × BODY AREA × GARMENT/EQUIPMENT × CURRENT SOLUTION × FAILURE × RESPONSE`

Then determine whether the event maps to S1, S2, S3, or another scenario.

### Priority 2 — Switching behavior

Measure:

- current solution
- previous solution
- actual switch
- trigger for switch
- whether the switch was topical → topical, topical → tape, topical → apparel/equipment, or reverse
- whether the runner returned to the old solution

Actual behavior should outrank hypothetical preference.

### Priority 3 — Economic relevance

Capture:

- current product price
- approximate amount/pack size
- purchases per year
- tape/apparel/equipment spending used specifically to solve the problem
- acceptable price range for a better solution
- purchase likelihood at standardized neutral prices

Do not infer WTP from a single open-ended maximum-price question alone.

### Priority 4 — Concept discrimination

Show neutralized S1/S2/S3 concept cards after the respondent has described their own experience.

Include:

- the option to keep current solution
- no exact duration
- no unsupported superiority statement
- no ingredient disclosure that biases selection
- standardized price where appropriate

Measure:

1. relevance to own problem
2. perceived usefulness
3. switching likelihood
4. purchase likelihood
5. reason for rejection

## Questions that should be removed or downgraded

The Korean survey should not spend substantial respondent time on:

- whether chafing exists at all;
- which anti-chafe brand is "best" in general;
- generic awareness of Body Glide/Vaseline/Squirrel's Nut Butter;
- exact product-duration claims;
- medical prevention/treatment outcomes;
- generic preference for stick vs cream before scenario context;
- detailed formulation ingredient preferences.

These either duplicate saturated discovery evidence or prematurely force a technical/product decision.

## Primary research minimum viable design

The study should be optimized for **decision discrimination**, not maximum sample size.

Minimum outputs:

1. S1/S2/S3 incidence within the recruited target sample
2. problem-intensity distribution by scenario
3. recurrence by scenario
4. actual switching by scenario
5. current workaround by scenario
6. topical vs non-topical substitution
7. spend and price sensitivity
8. neutral concept choice
9. contradictions and reasons
10. segment cells that materially change the ranking

Recruitment should deliberately include variation in:

- 10K / half / marathon / ultra experience
- typical run duration
- road / trail
- high vs low sweating
- female sports-bra interface where relevant
- users of topical / tape / apparel / no prevention

This is a **purposive decision sample**, not a national prevalence survey unless recruitment and weighting are separately designed to support that claim.

## Decision rule after Korean study

### SELECT
One scenario clearly leads on a combination of:
- problem intensity
- recurrence
- existing-solution gap
- actual switching
- economic relevance
- product solvability
- strategic differentiation

### CONDITIONAL SELECT
Two scenarios remain close, but one has a clear technical or commercial path that can be resolved with a limited next experiment or market check.

### HOLD
Evidence is insufficient or contradictory in a way that materially changes the concept ranking.

### REJECT
Scenario is interesting but lacks meaningful problem intensity, switching, economic relevance, or a product-solvable failure.

Scores are decision aids only; qualitative contradictions must remain visible.

## Current conclusion

The community/public-web evidence has already done the **problem discovery** work needed to justify a scenario-based endurance skin concept.

The Korean study should therefore be **small, targeted and comparative**. Its job is not to prove that chafing exists. Its job is to decide:

> **Which specific Korean endurance-running failure scenario deserves to become the first commercial product?**

Until that decision is made, do not lock formula, ingredient, format, package, duration claim, or technical test thresholds.

## Next action

Update the existing Korean runner field instrument so that it is explicitly organized around **S1/S2/S3 discrimination + actual switching + economics**, while retaining an open pathway for an unexpected fourth scenario.
