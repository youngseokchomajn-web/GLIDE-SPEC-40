# GLIDE-SPEC 40 — Product Form Factor & Manufacturing Assumptions

## Status
Rev.1 — 2026-09-14

This document records a clarification made after re-checking the intended physical product and publicly available market references. It is an interpretation/decision record, not a replacement for the formulation baseline.

## 1. Product form

GLIDE-SPEC 40 is currently defined as:

- **20 g solid stick-format skin anti-chafing product**
- Intended use: reduce skin friction and rubbing during running, marching and other prolonged activity
- Target finish: low-friction, relatively dry/powdery, low transfer, persistent film
- Fragrance: not required / currently excluded

The term **“Powder-in-Balm Stick” is not a confirmed commercial category name.** It should not be treated as a fixed product-class name or final manufacturing terminology.

## 2. Reference product class

A useful market reference for the *physical/formulation behavior* is **NUDESTIX Blot & Blur Matte Primer Stick**, which is described by the brand as a balm-to-powder stick. It is a cosmetic primer designed for oil/shine control and blur, not an anti-chafing product.

Therefore:

- It is a **formulation/texture reference**, not a GLIDE-SPEC 40 competitor definition.
- GLIDE-SPEC 40 should not be described as equivalent to this product.
- The relevant analogy is: a solid stick that applies smoothly like a balm and leaves a thinner, more powdery/dry surface.

## 3. Preferred technical description for manufacturers

Instead of asking manufacturers to make a “Powder-in-Balm Stick,” use a functional description:

> **20 g solid stick-format anti-chafing product. A wax/oil/silicone-based solid matrix containing dispersed fine particulate materials, designed to apply smoothly and form a thin, relatively dry/powdery surface film with reduced friction and controlled transfer.**

The final matrix and ingredient system remain subject to formulation development and pilot validation.

## 4. Relation to Rev.7.3 formulation baseline

Rev.7.3 currently contains a powder system consisting of BN, porous spherical silica, silica dimethyl silylate, PMSSQ and treated ZnO, with wax, silicone/carrier and resin systems providing the solid matrix.

That baseline is **not being declared commercially final by this document**. The purpose of this clarification is to describe the intended physical behavior more accurately and avoid treating “Powder-in-Balm” as a fixed industry category.

The powder system remains the major formulation/manufacturing risk and must be validated for:

1. powder wetting and dispersion
2. agglomeration
3. air entrapment
4. surface bloom/whitening
5. low-temperature pay-off
6. white cast
7. powder feel / friction feel
8. scale-up dispersion consistency

## 5. Manufacturing implications

Manufacturer screening should prioritize demonstrated experience with:

- solid stick filling
- anhydrous/oil-phase balm or stick systems
- balm-to-powder / matte stick / primer stick type textures
- dispersion of fine powders in solid stick matrices
- suitable 18–20 g stock stick packages
- pilot-scale filling before commercial production

The manufacturer does **not** need to recognize the phrase “Powder-in-Balm Stick” as a standard category if the technical behavior is clearly explained.

## 6. Initial production quantity

The previous assumption that **3,000 units is the minimum production quantity is removed.**

Current planning assumption:

> **Initial production quantity will be selected within approximately 1,000–3,000 units after checking manufacturer MOQ, formulation feasibility, package availability and actual quotations.**

A smaller pilot/technical batch may be required before the saleable first production run.

Publicly available domestic small-batch manufacturing examples show that some manufacturers/platforms advertise MOQ below 1,000 units, while others require 3,000–5,000+ units. These figures cannot be transferred directly to GLIDE-SPEC 40 because the specific stick formulation and package must be accepted by the manufacturer.

## 7. Cost assumption

The previous **2,950 KRW/unit** figure is retained only as a **target COGS**, not as an established manufacturing cost.

It must be validated through actual quotations.

Business-planning ranges may be used provisionally, but should be explicitly labeled as estimates until manufacturers quote the complete product under the same specification.

## 8. Manufacturer quotation request

The first quotation round should request the same specification at:

- 1,000 units
- 2,000 units
- 3,000 units

Where feasible, also ask whether 500-unit technical/pilot production is possible.

Request separate pricing for:

- formulation/development
- pilot/sample work
- bulk manufacturing
- filling
- stock stick package
- labeling/printing
- secondary packaging
- QC/testing
- other setup/tooling costs

Also request:

- minimum order quantity
- stock package options in the 18–20 g range
- compatibility with the proposed formulation
- pilot availability
- expected development lead time
- mass-production lead time

## 9. Decision rule

Until actual manufacturer feedback is obtained:

- **Do not state 3,000 units as the MOQ.**
- **Do not state 2,950 KRW/unit as actual COGS.**
- **Do not lock a dedicated package mold.**
- **Do not treat “Powder-in-Balm Stick” as a final technical category name.**
- Use existing stock stick packages as the first-choice package path.

The immediate next step is manufacturer feasibility and quotation validation, not further refinement of an assumed MOQ or assumed unit cost.
