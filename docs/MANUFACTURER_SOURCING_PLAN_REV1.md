# GLIDE-SPEC 40 — Manufacturer Sourcing Plan

## Status
Rev.1 — 2026-09-14

## Objective

Identify a Korean cosmetic OEM/ODM partner that can actually develop and fill the GLIDE-SPEC 40 product, then validate MOQ, development cost, production cost and lead time with comparable quotations.

The goal is **not** to find the manufacturer with the lowest advertised MOQ. The goal is to find a manufacturer capable of reproducing the required stick behavior consistently at a commercially sensible first-batch size.

## 1. Current product brief for manufacturer screening

- Format: 20 g solid stick
- Function: skin anti-chafing / friction-reduction product
- Intended use: repeated skin friction during running, marching and other prolonged activity
- Desired application: smooth glide during application
- Desired after-feel: thin, relatively dry/powdery, low-tack surface
- Desired performance: reduced friction, controlled transfer, persistence under activity/sweat
- Formulation direction: anhydrous/oil/wax/silicone solid matrix with dispersed fine particulate materials
- Package direction: stock 18–20 g twist-up stick; no dedicated mold at this stage
- Reference behavior: balm-to-powder/matte stick products may be shown as texture references, but GLIDE-SPEC 40 is not a facial primer or equivalent product

Do not use “Powder-in-Balm Stick” as if it were a standardized industry category. Explain the required physical behavior instead.

## 2. Search strategy

### Tier A — first priority
Manufacturers with evidence of:
- solid stick production/filling
- sun-stick, balm-stick, primer-stick, matte-stick or similar experience
- anhydrous/oil-phase formulation
- fine powder dispersion
- in-house formulation/R&D
- pilot or small-batch capability

### Tier B — comparison candidates
Manufacturers with strong small-batch OEM/ODM capability but insufficient public evidence of the exact stick/powder formulation. Contact only if they confirm technical feasibility.

### Tier C — technical/scale-up candidates
Larger ODM/OEM manufacturers with demonstrated stick technology but potentially higher MOQ. Keep as a technical benchmark and future scale-up option rather than assuming they fit the first production run.

## 5. Important negative evidence

Some public examples show that stick production can have materially higher MOQ. For example, public material reviewed for one multi-balm/sun-stick OEM route states a 5,000-unit MOQ, while Hanform publicly states a 5,000-unit basic MOQ. Therefore the project should not assume that every stick manufacturer can support 1,000 units.

The correct conclusion is:

> **MOQ is manufacturer-, formulation-, filling-line- and package-dependent. The 1,000–3,000 unit range is a planning range to test, not a guaranteed manufacturing MOQ.**

## 7. Questions that determine technical fit

1. Have you manufactured solid balm/stick products before?
2. Have you filled sun sticks, multi-balms, primer/matte sticks or similar formats?
3. Have you manufactured anhydrous/oil/wax/silicone stick systems?
4. Do you have experience dispersing silica, boron nitride, PMSSQ, ZnO or similar fine powders in a stick matrix?
5. Can you perform pilot filling before the first saleable production run?
6. Can you recommend a stock 18–20 g twist-up package already compatible with your filling line?
7. What is the smallest practical batch for this specific formulation, not your generic cosmetic MOQ?
8. What problems do you expect with powder dispersion, air entrapment, bloom/whitening, pay-off or stick breakage?
9. What process controls are used for batch-to-batch consistency?
10. Can the proposed formulation be modified after pilot testing without restarting the entire development process?

## 10. Pilot decision gate

Do not select a final manufacturer from quotation alone.

A candidate should advance only if it can demonstrate:

- acceptable stick formation
- consistent pay-off
- no unacceptable powder agglomeration
- acceptable surface appearance
- acceptable glide/friction behavior
- acceptable transfer
- acceptable stick mechanical strength
- acceptable packaging compatibility

Only after this gate should the project choose between 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 saleable units.

## 11. Current execution order

1. Contact Priority A candidates.
2. Run the same RFQ for all candidates.
3. Record every answer in a comparison table.
4. Reject candidates that cannot confirm stick filling or technical feasibility.
5. Request sample/Pilot proposals from the top three.
6. Compare samples using the same GLIDE-SPEC 40 test protocol.
7. Select one primary and one backup manufacturer.
8. Decide first saleable production quantity from actual quotation + pilot result.
9. Update the target COGS only after real quotations are obtained.

## 12. Evidence discipline

Public MOQ statements are evidence of the manufacturer's advertised policy, not proof that GLIDE-SPEC 40 can be produced at that MOQ.

Public product examples are evidence of format/formulation experience, not proof of identical technical capability.

Every manufacturer record should distinguish:

- **PUBLICLY CONFIRMED**
- **MANUFACTURER CLAIM — NEEDS DIRECT CONFIRMATION**
- **OUR INFERENCE**
- **DIRECT QUOTATION / PILOT RESULT**

The final manufacturing decision should be based on the last category wherever possible.
